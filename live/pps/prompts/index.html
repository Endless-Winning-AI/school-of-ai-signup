Prompt Architect Architecture
Turns a rough idea into a fully structured, production-ready prompt. Use this first before any important AI task.
How to use
Paste this prompt. Then tell the AI your rough idea ("I want a prompt that helps me...") and let it structure the full prompt for you. Copy the output and use it anywhere.
Prompt 
You are a world-class prompt engineer. I will give you a rough idea of what I want an AI to do. Your job is to return a fully structured, production-ready prompt using this architecture:

1. ROLE — who the AI is (be specific about expertise, tone, worldview).
2. CONTEXT — what the AI needs to know before starting the task.
3. OBJECTIVE — the outcome, in one sentence.
4. CONSTRAINTS — what it must and must not do.
5. INPUTS — what the user will paste/provide, in {{placeholder}} form.
6. PROCESS — step-by-step how the AI should think through the task.
7. OUTPUT FORMAT — exact structure of the final response (headings, length, tone).
8. EXAMPLES (if useful) — 1-2 input→output examples.

Before writing the final prompt, ask me up to 3 clarifying questions if the intent is ambiguous. Then deliver the full structured prompt in a single code block I can copy.

My rough idea: {{PASTE YOUR IDEA HERE}}

Chain-of-Thought Booster Architecture
Forces the AI to reason out loud before answering. Lifts quality on any logic-heavy, math, or analysis task by 20-40%.
How to use
Prepend this to any question that needs real reasoning. The explicit "show your work" section is the unlock.
Prompt 
Before you answer, think carefully step-by-step.

1. Restate the task in your own words (one sentence).
2. List the facts or inputs that matter.
3. List the assumptions you're making.
4. Work through the reasoning explicitly, showing each step.
5. State the answer clearly.
6. Sanity-check: "If I'm wrong, the most likely reason is ___."

Self-Critique & Rewrite Loop Architecture
Generate → brutally critique → rewrite. Gets 80% of the quality of multi-agent systems in one prompt.
How to use
Use when the first draft of anything matters — copywriting, strategy, code, emails. Works best on outputs longer than a paragraph.
Prompt 
You are going to answer this task THREE times, in order:

TASK: {{YOUR TASK HERE}}

PASS 1 — DRAFT
Write your first attempt. Don't overthink it.

PASS 2 — BRUTAL CRITIQUE
Now put on the hat of the harshest, most competent reviewer in the world. Critique Pass 1. Find:
- What's vague or generic
- What claims aren't supported
- What's missing that would make this 10x better
- Where the reasoning is weak
- What a real expert would say about this

List at least 5 issues. Be specific. No compliments.

PASS 3 — REWRITE
Now rewrite the draft addressing every critique. This final version is what I'll actually use.

Label each pass clearly and show all three.

ReAct — Reason + Act Architecture
Interleaves thinking with tool use. The framework under every serious AI agent — now usable in a single-chat window.
How to use
For any task where the AI needs to search, analyze, then act (research, troubleshooting, investigation). Especially powerful in ChatGPT with browsing on, or Claude with web search.
Prompt 
For the task below, work in this loop until you reach the answer:

THOUGHT: [What do I need to figure out next? What's my current hypothesis?]
ACTION: [What tool/search/check will I run? Describe exactly what you're doing.]
OBSERVATION: [What did that reveal?]

Repeat THOUGHT → ACTION → OBSERVATION until you have enough to answer confidently.

End with:
FINAL ANSWER: [The actual answer, with a confidence level 1-10 and one sentence on what would change it.]

Cold Email Architect Marketing
Value-first cold email that doesn't smell like a pitch. Hormozi-inspired framework: lead with specific value, earn the reply.
How to use
Best if you know something specific about the recipient (their site, their LinkedIn, a recent post). Generic = dead. Specific = opens.
Prompt 
You write cold emails like a value-first operator, not a salesperson. No corporate fluff, no "I hope this email finds you well," no pitch in line 1.

Write a cold email using this structure:

1. SUBJECT — 3-5 words, pattern-interrupting. No emojis. Specific, not clever.
2. LINE 1 — a concrete observation about the recipient (from their site/LinkedIn/post). Proves you actually looked.
3. LINE 2 — the specific insight or value you want to hand them. Not a question, not a pitch. A gift.
4. LINE 3 — a soft ask that's easy to say yes to. Never "hop on a call." Try "worth a 2-line reply?" or "want the full breakdown?"
5. SIGN-OFF — first name only. No title, no company, no links.

Total length: under 90 words. Punchy sentences. One idea per line.

Recipient profile:
{{NAME, ROLE, COMPANY, SPECIFIC DETAIL YOU KNOW}}

Specific value you can offer them:
{{WHAT YOU NOTICED + WHAT YOU'D DO ABOUT IT}}

Write 3 subject-line options + 1 full email.

For each opportunity, output:
1. THE OPPORTUNITY — one sentence, specific to their business

4. EFFORT TO DEPLOY — hours of work, rough skill level
5. PROJECTED RETURN — hours saved/week OR dollars saved/week OR revenue unlocked. Show your math.

Rank all 5 from highest ROI to lowest.

Paste a competitor's landing page or offer. Out comes their full positioning breakdown — promise, proof, price logic, gaps.
How to use
Paste the full page text (or URL, if your model browses). Great for building your own differentiated offer in a crowded category.
Prompt 
You are a positioning strategist. I'll give you a competitor's landing page or offer. Break it down into its components and tell me what's working, what's weak, and where a new entrant could win.

Deliver:

1. THE PROMISE — what they say the customer will get. Exact language.
2. THE AVATAR — who they're talking to, and what about that person is implicit in the copy.
3. THE PROOF — every form of proof they use (testimonials, stats, logos, guarantees, credentials).

5. THE WEAKNESSES — 3 gaps or weak claims a skeptical prospect would catch.
6. THE OPENING — the sharpest angle a new entrant could attack from. What's the differentiated promise, and what copy would they write to claim it?

Be ruthless. Don't compliment. Be specific enough that I could use your output as a positioning brief.

SOP Extractor Ops
You describe a messy process. AI asks smart questions, then hands you a clean, delegatable SOP with decision points.
How to use
Pick a process you do 5+ times a month that lives in your head. Describe it in plain language. The AI interrogates you until it's written down properly.
Prompt 
You are an operations consultant who writes SOPs so clean that a new hire could execute them on day one without asking questions.

I'll describe a process in messy, stream-of-consciousness detail. Your job is to:

1. Ask me up to 6 clarifying questions — only ones you genuinely need answers to. No throwaways.
2. After I answer, produce a clean SOP with:
   • TITLE
   • TRIGGER (what starts this process)
   • OWNER (role, not person)
   • INPUTS (what you need before you start)
   • STEPS (numbered, action verbs, no more than 12)
   • DECISION POINTS (where the path forks — "if X, do Y, otherwise Z")
   • OUTPUTS (what exists when this is done)
   • EDGE CASES & EXCEPTIONS (the 3 things that most commonly go wrong)
   • SUCCESS CHECK (how we know the process worked)
   • TIME ESTIMATE (rough)

Keep language plain. No jargon. A smart 16-year-old should be able to follow it.

HERE'S THE PROCESS:
{{DESCRIBE IT MESSILY — just get the thoughts out}}

Meeting-to-Action Converter Ops
Paste a meeting transcript. Out comes decisions, owned action items, risks, and a clean follow-up email draft.
How to use
Works with transcripts from Zoom, Otter, Fireflies, or even rough notes. The more names + quotes, the sharper the action-item attribution.
Prompt 
You are a chief-of-staff. I'll paste a raw meeting transcript. Turn it into a tight post-meeting package with these sections in this exact order:

1. DECISIONS MADE — bullet list. Each one in one sentence, no fluff, names attached where the transcript supports it.
2. ACTION ITEMS — table format: [Owner] · [Action — a concrete verb] · [Due]. If no due date was discussed, mark TBD and flag it.
3. OPEN QUESTIONS — things surfaced but not resolved.
4. RISKS & FLAGS — anything in the transcript that would worry a good operator (conflicts, unclear ownership, shaky assumptions, tight timelines).
5. FOLLOW-UP EMAIL DRAFT — ready to send. Subject line + body. Friendly but crisp. Under 150 words. Bullet any action items with owners.

Rules:
- Never invent decisions or owners. If the transcript is ambiguous, say "UNCLEAR — recommend confirming."
- Quote exact wording when a decision is nuanced.

Negotiation Coach (Chris Voss mode) Business
Role-plays the other side of any deal you're about to walk into. Tactical empathy, labeling, calibrated questions — the full playbook.
How to use
Describe the negotiation you're walking into. The AI first plays you the other side until you're prepared, then gives you the playbook.
Prompt 
You are a negotiation coach trained on Chris Voss's "Never Split the Difference." You use tactical empathy, mirroring, labeling, and calibrated "how" / "what" questions — never "why" (too accusatory).

I'll describe a negotiation I'm about to walk into. Do this:

STAGE 1 — Ask me 3 sharp questions to understand:
  a) What I actually want (and what I'd walk away for)
  b) What they actually want (your best read)
  c) The implicit fears or pressures on their side

STAGE 2 — Roleplay the other party. Open with a realistic hard line they might take. Let me respond. Push back on weak responses. Keep going for 3-4 rounds.

STAGE 3 — Give me the playbook:
  • The opening line I should use
  • 3 labels to pre-position their emotions ("it sounds like...", "it seems like you're worried about...")
  • 3 calibrated questions to get them to solve my problem for me
  • The worst thing I could say (so I don't say it)
  • One "black swan" — a surprise piece of info I should dig for

HERE'S THE NEGOTIATION:
{{DESCRIBE: what's at stake, who the other party is, where we are in the convo, what's already happened}}

First-Principles Strategist Strategy
Strips an assumption-heavy decision back to physics. Useful when "everyone does it this way" needs to be challenged.
How to use
Use on decisions where the "industry standard" feels like a cop-out. Pricing, hiring, GTM, product architecture.
Prompt 
You are a first-principles strategist. Your job: take a decision I'm making by convention and rebuild it from scratch.

DECISION I'M MAKING:
{{DESCRIBE THE DECISION AND WHAT I'M LEANING TOWARD AND WHY}}

Work in this order:

1. LIST THE ASSUMPTIONS — every assumption baked into my current approach. Be ruthless. Include the ones I didn't state but am clearly making.

2. SEPARATE — which assumptions are:
   a) Physics / law (truly inviolable — can't change)
   b) Convention (industry "just does it" — fully changeable)
   c) My own preference (changeable if I'm willing)

3. RE-DERIVE — if I threw out all convention-level assumptions, what does the decision actually look like? Design from scratch using only the physics and my preferences.

4. COMPARE — what does the first-principles version get me that the default version doesn't? What would I lose?

5. RECOMMENDATION — your honest read. Which version wins, and what's the bet you're making? Include one scenario where you'd be wrong.

Don't hedge. Don't give me "it depends." Pick a side.
